\begin{abstract}
This document introduces the charging behaviour of a resistor-capacitor (RC) network and derives the capacitor voltage at one time constant.    
\end{abstract}
\section{Introduction}
This document introduces the charging behaviour present in a resistor-capacitor (RC) circuit. When a capacitor $C$ is charged through a series resistor $R$ from a constant supply  $V_0$, the capacitor voltage rises according to
\begin{equation}
    V(t) = V_0 (1 - e^{-t/RC}),
    \label{eq:Charge equation}
\end{equation}
where the product RC has units of seconds and is called the time constant, $\tau = RC$
\section{Voltage at the Time Constant}
Substituing t = $\tau$ into (\ref{eq:Charge equation}):
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    V(\tau) &= V_0 \left(1-e^{-\tau/RC}\right) \\
             &= V_0 \left(1-e^{-RC/RC}\right)
                \qquad \text{since } \tau = RC \\
             &= V_0 \left(1-e^{-1}\right) \\
             &\approx V_0(1-0.368) \\
             &\approx 0.632V_0.
             \label{eq:VoltageTau}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The exponent cancelling to $-1$ in (\ref{eq:VoltageTau}) is the entire reason
the 0.632 fraction is universal — R and C dropped out of
the calculation before the final line, so this fraction holds
for any RC network. Substituting the lab’s supply voltage
$V_0 = 5.0 V$ 
\begin{equation}
    V(\tau) \approx 0.632  \times 5.0V \approx 3.16 V
\end{equation}
