\begin{abstract}
This document derives the charging behaviour of a resistor-capacitor (RC) network, the voltage reached after one time constant, and applies the result to size a power-on-reset(POR) circuit for a microcontroller with an active-low reset.  
\end{abstract}
\section{Introduction}
When a capacitor $C$ is charged through a series resistor $R$ from a constant supply $V_0$, the capacitor voltage rises according to
\begin{equation}
    V(t) = V_0 (1 - e^{-t/RC}),
    \label{eq:Charge equation}
\end{equation}
where $\tau = RC$ is the time constant.Substituting t = $\tau$ gives $V(\tau) = V_0(1-e^{-1}) \approx 0.632 V_0$.

\section{Power On Reset with An active Low Reset}
A power-on-reset circuit exploits (\ref{eq:Charge equation}) directly: $R$ from $V_{CC}$ to the reset node and $C$ from that node to ground hold the microcontroller`s active-low reset pin low until the node crosses $V_{1H}$, releasing the device

\subsection{Sizing $R$ for a timing Constraint}
The reset pin releases the instant the node voltage reaches $V_1H$. Setting $V(t_{release}) = V_{1H}$ in (\ref{eq:Charge equation}) and solving for $t{release}:$
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        & V_{1H} = V_0 ( 1 - e^{-t_{release}/RC}\\
        &\frac{V_{1H}}{V_0} = 1 - e^{-trelease/RC}\\
        &e^{-trelease/RC} = 1 - \frac{V_{1H}}{V_0}\\
        &-\frac{t_{release}}{RC} = ln(1 - \frac{V{1H}}{V_0})\\
        &t_{release} = -RC ln(1- \frac{V_{1H}}{V_0})
    \end{aligned}
    \label{R for a time constant}
\end{equation}
Rearranging (\ref{R for a time constant}) for R, given a target $t_{release}$ and a chosen $C$:
\begin{equation}
    R = \frac{-t_{release}}{C ln(1 -\frac{V_{1H}}{V_0})}
    \label{R given trelease}
\end{equation}
1) \textit{Worked example:} Take $V_{IH} = 0.7 V_0, V_0 = 5.0 V$, and a target $T_{release} = 10ms$. The $C = 22 pF$ used for the plots above was chosen only to keep the curves readable at a submicrosecond timescale; a practical timing design instead starts from $C = 100nf$.Evaluating the denominator of (\ref{R given trelease}):
\begin{equation}
    ln(1-0.7) = ln(0.3) \approx -1.204.
\end{equation}
Substituting into (\ref{R given trelease}):
\begin{equation}
    R = \frac{-(10 \times 10^{-3})}{(100 \times 10^{-9}) \times (-1.204)} \approx 83 k\Omega
\end{equation}
An $83k\Omega$ resistor with a $100 nF$ capacitor gives a reset hold-off of approximately 10 ms,Keeping $C$ at the plotting value.
\section{Applications and Limitations}
\subsection{Application}
A simple 25-minute electronic timer. The application works in this case because the user won't notice that the timer starts a couple of milliseconds after it is turned on, and the circuit is low-power, so there are no spikes or serious voltage drops. For this application, use the values $C = 1 uF$ and $R = 16K\Omega$, resulting in $trelease \approx 20ms$.
\begin{equation}
    trelease = -(16k\Omega \times 1 uF) \times -1.204 \approx 20ms
    \label{Trelease for Application}
\end{equation}
\subsection{Limitations}
The system may fail if the battery or power source runs low and does not provide the necessary power; the initial startup time may vary, affecting the quality of the reset, and the device can only be reset by turning it off and then back on. Based on the typical tolerances the capacitor would have $+/-20\%$ and the resistor would have $+/- 5\%$
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\delta t}{t} \approx 0.5 + 0.20 = 0.25
\end{equation}
True release would be around $15ms$ and $25ms$
